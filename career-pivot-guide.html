CAREER DEVELOPMENT GUIDE

A career pivot is a project. Treat it like one.

Changing roles is easier to reason about when you break it into target definition, skill evidence, focused practice and opportunity management.

1. Define a target role

Choose one role to investigate first. Read several current job descriptions from employers you would realistically apply to. Separate essential requirements from preferred ones. Record the skills, tools, responsibilities and evidence repeatedly requested.

2. Assess your current evidence

List the skills you can demonstrate today. A skill rating is only a starting point; link it to a project, coursework, work sample, open-source contribution or measurable outcome. Be explicit about what you personally contributed.

3. Prioritize the gaps

Rank gaps by relevance to the target role, current capability and effort to produce credible evidence. Avoid trying to learn every tool at once. Pick a small number of high-impact skills for the next learning cycle.

4. Build proof through projects

Prefer finished, documented work over a long list of unfinished tutorials. Explain the problem, constraints, decisions, implementation, tests and result. Do not invent metrics or claim team outcomes as individual work.

5. Practise explaining your decisions

Use a clear structure for behavioral questions: context, task, action, result and reflection. For technical interviews, practise explaining trade-offs and debugging steps rather than memorizing definitions alone.

6. Manage applications deliberately

Track role, company, date, status, next action and follow-up. Review outcomes weekly and adjust the plan based on evidence, not one rejection or one interview.

7. Review the plan every two weeks

Measure completed milestones, stronger evidence and interview practice. Update the target when credible market information or personal constraints change.

Important limitationThis framework is general career-planning guidance. CareerOS does not guarantee employment, validate psychometric aptitude or provide live labour-market research.
